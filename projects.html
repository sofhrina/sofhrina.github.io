May – Jun 2026 · Python, Matplotlib · UCL Year 1

Algorithms and Big-O

I implemented bubble sort, merge sort, quicksort, and counting sort from scratch, then designed timing experiments across inputs from 100 to 10,000 elements. A logarithmic plot made the separation between quadratic, linearithmic, and linear-plus-range growth visible.

The lasting part was reconstructing the decision-tree lower-bound argument in English. It taught me the difference between following a proof and being able to rebuild it.

Jan – Mar 2026 · UCL Data Science Society · rebuilding

Cross-sectional alpha strategy, auto sector

A ranking pipeline over nine global automotive equities using momentum, volatility, and RSI factors, a depth-constrained Random Forest, and Spearman rank IC.

I am rewriting it from scratch. The original Sharpe ratio is high enough to suggest a bug—most likely look-ahead bias or missing transaction costs—and I want to understand every line before putting my name on the result.

Feb – Mar 2026 · UCL, Cambridge, LSE, Imperial

Strategic M&A valuation

Our cross-university team acted for Samsung Electronics, selected Lattice Semiconductor as an acquisition target, and built the valuation case. I led the quantitative work: a five-year forecast, CAPM-based WACC, DCF, and comparable-company ranges.

The methods disagreed sharply. Learning to explain why a control premium could still be defensible taught me more than making the spreadsheet agree with itself.

Apr 2026 · seven-person cross-university team

AI wearable concept

A companion wearable concept for anxiety detection in university students. I handled market sizing, competitor mapping, gaps in existing products, and the route by which the device might reach the people it was intended to help.

It was the first time I had to argue for something without a proof behind it, and I found the ambiguity harder than the mathematics.
